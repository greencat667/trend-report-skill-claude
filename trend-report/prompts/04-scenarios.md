# Scenarios

> **Section 4** | Target: ~350 words (±5%) | Pause for review after drafting

## Prompt

Draft **Section 4: Scenarios** (~350 words) for the report on **{{TOPIC}}**.

The main body includes the **lead scenario (Scenario A)** in full. Scenarios B and C go in Appendix 3.

### Structure

1. **Introduction** (≤50 words) — Explain that we considered three scenarios:
   - An optimistic, transformative future
   - A conflicted, transitional future
   - A challenging, cautionary future

2. **Scenario A: [Title]** (≤300 words) — The optimistic/transformative scenario
   - Write as a **narrative vignette** set **at least 10 years from present day** (e.g. 2036–2040). This is not a near-future extrapolation — it should feel like a genuinely different world.
   - Name a protagonist with a specific role and location
   - Use sensory details (sights, sounds, smells) to bring the scene alive
   - Show the system working well: how has infrastructure, technology, and civic engagement evolved?
   - End with a moment of reflection that captures the transformation
   - **Balancing imagination with groundedness:** The vignette should provoke the reader's imagination without feeling like science fiction. Root it in real places, plausible institutions, and recognisable human situations. The protagonist should have a job, a commute, a community — not a utopia. Show change through mundane details (what's on the noticeboard, what the kids are learning, what's different about the high street) rather than spectacular technology. If a reader who thinks literally can see themselves in the scene, you've got it right.

3. **Closing pointer** — Direct readers to the appendix for Scenarios B and C.

## Style Rules

- Write as a colleague, not a consultant. First person plural ("we") in framing text around the vignettes.
- Present tense for the vignette ("It's a crisp October morning...")
- Concrete, specific details over abstract statements
- Prioritise emotional directness over system description. Show how the change *feels*, not just how it works.
- Keep exposition minimal — if a policy or scheme needs explaining, do it through dialogue or action, not narration.
- Show, don't tell: demonstrate the future through actions and dialogue
- At least two lines of dialogue, kept natural and clipped (not speechy)
- UK-specific setting preferred
- Avoid consultant-speak in framing text: don't write "paradigm shift", "transformative potential", "systemic reconfiguration". Say what changed and how it feels. Before/after example: ❌ "The transformative potential of community-led governance has been fully realised" → ✅ "The council doesn't decide on its own any more. Residents vote on the big stuff — and they actually show up."
- **No meta-text:** Do not include word counts, drafting notes, or other artefacts in your output.
