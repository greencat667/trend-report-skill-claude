# Executive Summary

> **Section 0** | Target: ~400 words (±5%) | Pause for review after drafting

## Prompt

Draft the **Executive Summary** (~400 words) for a trend report on **{{TOPIC}}**.

The summary must include these components in order:

### 0. Framing (≤30 words)
One or two sentences positioning this as a working document that invites discussion, not a finished analysis. E.g. "This report is intended to provoke discussion, not settle it."

### 1. Why this matters now (≤80 words)
A compelling opening that explains why this trend demands attention *right now*. Use concrete, recent shifts—not abstract importance. Include a brief **relative urgency assessment**: is this a watch-and-learn topic, an emerging priority, or a live operational concern for {{ORG}}? Calibrate the tone accordingly — not everything is a five-alarm fire. Help the reader judge where this sits against other demands on the organisation's attention.

### 2. What we found (≤120 words)
Summarise the key findings from signals/drivers mapping, SWOT analysis, and scenario work. Be specific about the balance of forces (e.g. "dominated by legal and technological change, with social responses under-developed").

### 3. Strategic implications (≤100 words)
What does this mean for {{ORG}}? Route implications through the organisation's **strategic pillars** (from PROJECT.md). For each pillar that is materially affected, state the implication in one sentence. Prioritise whichever pillar sits closest to the organisation's core theory of change — for a campaigning organisation this is often the pillar about building power or influence; for others it may be delivery, membership, or something else entirely. Frame as risks of inaction and opportunities for leadership. Use bullet points if helpful.

### 4. Learning priorities for Phase 2 (≤100 words)
List 3 learning priorities for further exploration. Each should be a short phrase with a one-line explanation.

## Style Rules

- Write as a colleague, not a consultant. First person plural ("we", "our").
- Plain, direct English. Short sentences (target under 15 words). Break long sentences in two.
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- No jargon without explanation; prefer everyday words over policy vocabulary
- Avoid consultant-speak: don't write "strategic landscape", "paradigm shift", "multi-stakeholder approach". Say what's happening and what it means for us. Before/after example: ❌ "The strategic landscape presents both risks and opportunities for organisational positioning" → ✅ "This is moving fast. If we don't act, others will set the terms."
- Define technical terms briefly on first use — assume the reader is smart but not a specialist
- Cite sources inline as `([Org Name](https://url))` if referencing specific data. All citations must be parenthesized linked markdown — no unlinked source names.
- End with a punchy closing sentence
- **No meta-text:** Do not include word counts, drafting notes, or other artefacts in your output.
