# Appendix 5: Three Horizons

> **Appendix A5** | Target: ~860 words (±5%) | Pause for review after drafting

## Prompt

Draft **Appendix 5: Three Horizons Exploration** (~800 words) for the report on **{{TOPIC}}**.

Use the Three Horizons framework to map transition from present to future. **Horizon 3 is the centrepiece of this section** — it should receive the most space and the most imaginative attention.

### Framing paragraph (required, ~60 words)

Open with a brief explanation of the Three Horizons framework in plain language. Key points to cover:

- The three horizons are not sequential timeframes. **All three coexist at any moment.** H1 is the dominant system, H2 represents emerging alternatives that challenge it, and H3 is the seeds of a fundamentally different future that are already being planted.
- The framework helps us see where the current system is losing fitness, where new possibilities are emerging, and what kind of future we want to build toward.

### Structure

#### Horizon 1 — The dominant system (~150 words)
- Describe the current dominant system
- Who holds power? What are the rules?
- What are the strengths and vulnerabilities?
- What keeps the current system stable?
- Note where cracks are appearing

#### Horizon 2 — Transitional tensions (emerging alternatives) (~200 words)
- What new approaches are being tested?
- Where is friction arising between old and new?
- What competing logics are in play? (e.g. centralised vs decentralised, automated vs human)
- What sustainability/safety challenges do emerging approaches face?
- Where are the tipping points — what would tip the balance from H1 to H3?

#### Horizon 3 — Visionary future (transformed system) (~350 words)

This is the heart of the section. Give it room. H3 should:
- Paint a detailed, specific picture of the fully transformed state — not a vague aspiration but a world the reader can walk through
- Describe what daily life looks like for ordinary people (not just policymakers or campaigners)
- Show how the system works differently: who holds power, how decisions get made, what the rules are
- Address how current harms have been mitigated — be specific about mechanisms, not just outcomes
- Name what role {{ORG}} plays in this future
- What does success look like for communities?
- Connect back to weak signals and seeds that are already planted today — show that H3 is not fantasy but an extension of things already happening
- End on an aspirational but grounded note

**Tone for H3:** This should feel like a plausible, desirable future — not a utopia. Include texture: things that are still imperfect, trade-offs that were made, debates that continue. A future with no remaining tensions is not believable. The reader should finish H3 thinking "that's a world worth building" rather than "that's nice but impossible."

#### Connecting thread (~100 words)
- A closing paragraph that links the three horizons together
- Name the choices and interventions that determine whether we move from H1 through H2 to H3 — or get stuck
- What does {{ORG}} need to do *now* to plant the seeds of H3?

### Framework Reference

| Horizon | Character | Examples | Word budget |
|---------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| H1 | Dominant system — established, stable but losing fitness | Current rules, incumbent actors, mainstream assumptions | ~150 words |
| H2 | Emerging alternatives — contested, gaining traction | Pilots, policy experiments, new coalitions, competing logics | ~200 words |
| H3 | Seeds of transformation — visionary, already being planted | Weak signals, radical experiments, reimagined systems | ~350 words |

> **Important:** Do not peg horizons to specific year ranges. All three horizons are present simultaneously. H1 features exist alongside H3 seeds right now. The horizons describe *types of activity*, not *periods of time*.

## Style Rules

- Write as a colleague, not a consultant. First person plural ("we", "our").
- Plain, direct English. Short sentences (target under 15 words). Break long sentences in two.
- Avoid consultant-speak: don't write "the systems analysis shows", "strategic implications", "reinforcing dynamics". Say what's actually happening and why it matters to us. Before/after example: ❌ "Reinforcing loops between housing commodification and deregulation drive systemic inequality" → ✅ "Housing gets more expensive because deregulation lets developers build what sells, not what people need."
- Define technical terms briefly on first use — assume the reader is smart but not a specialist
- Present tense throughout
- Be specific to UK context
- Connect back to signals and drivers (especially weak signals for H3)
- Balance realism with aspiration in H3 — include imperfections and trade-offs
- **Citations:** Where you reference specific data, policies, or organisations, cite inline as `([Source Name](https://url))`. H3 (visionary future) does not need citations, but H1 and H2 should cite material claims. Do not include a loose "Sources" list at the end — every citation must be inline and linked.
- **No meta-text:** Do not include word counts, "Sources" dump sections, drafting notes, or other artefacts in your output.
