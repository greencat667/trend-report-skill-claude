# Appendix 1: Signals & Drivers (Detailed)

> **Appendix A1** | Target: ~800 words (±5%) | Pause for review after drafting

## Prompt

Draft **Appendix 1: Signals & Drivers** (~800 words) for the report on **{{TOPIC}}**.

This is the full PESTEL analysis with citations, plus a weak signals scan for longer-term developments.

### Structure

#### Part 1: Current signals (≤200 words total)

List **8-10 emerging signals** as bullet points. Each signal should:
- Describe a specific, recent event, initiative, or development (last 1-2 years)
- Note geography in parentheses where relevant
- End with a parenthesized linked citation: `([Org Name](https://url))`
- Be 15-25 words

Focus on variety: policy, tech, civic, international examples.

#### Part 1b: Weak signals (≤200 words total)

List **4-6 weak signals** — early-stage developments that won't mature for 7-15 years but could reshape the landscape. These are things in labs, small pilots, academic papers, niche communities, or policy margins — not yet in the news cycle. Each signal should:
- Describe a specific development, experiment, or emerging idea
- Note why it matters at scale (what changes if this works?)
- Note geography in parentheses where relevant
- End with a parenthesized linked citation: `([Org Name](https://url))`
- Be 20-30 words

Search beyond mainstream news: look at academic preprints, niche trade publications, patent filings, government foresight reports, and small-scale pilots. These signals should feel surprising or unfamiliar to most readers.

#### Part 2: Macro-drivers — PESTEL (≤400 words total)

Organise under 6 headings. For each relevant driver:
- **Bold the driver name**
- Tag with horizon using the Three Horizons framework: `(H1)` = dominant system (established, mainstream), `(H2)` = emerging alternatives (contested, gaining traction), `(H3)` = seeds of transformation (visionary, early-stage). These are not timeframes — all three horizons coexist at any moment. A driver is H1 if it reinforces the current system, H2 if it disrupts it, H3 if it points toward a fundamentally different future.
- Write 40-60 words explaining the driver
- Include at least one citation

**Headings:**
- **Political** — Government action, regulation, election dynamics
- **Economic** — Costs, incentives, business models
- **Social** — Trust, civic behaviour, cultural shifts
- **Technological** — Tools, platforms, AI, provenance
- **Environmental** — Climate-specific drivers (if relevant)
- **Legal** — Liability, enforcement, international frameworks

Not all headings need content — skip if not relevant.

#### Part 3: Analysis summary (≤50 words)

A brief paragraph noting patterns: which domains dominate? What's underdeveloped? Where are UK leverage points?

## Style Rules

- Write as a colleague, not a consultant. First person plural ("we", "our").
- Plain, direct English. Short sentences (target under 15 words). Break long sentences in two.
- Define technical terms briefly on first use — assume the reader is smart but not a specialist
- Avoid consultant-speak in driver explanations: don't write "regulatory frameworks", "market dynamics", "stakeholder ecosystems". Name the actual rules, money flows, or people involved. Before/after example: ❌ "Evolving regulatory frameworks are reshaping market dynamics in the platform economy" → ✅ "The EU's new rules force platforms to label AI-generated content. The UK hasn't followed yet."
- All citations must be real and verifiable
- Use parenthesized linked markdown citations only: `([Source](https://...))`
- Do not use unlinked source names
- Keep bullet lists strict markdown (blank line before list, one item per line)
- **No meta-text:** Do not include word counts, drafting notes, or other artefacts in your output.
